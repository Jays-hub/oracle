## 8. Beyond the template — the cold-start problem

*The template has no row for this, and it is the most important thing the call produced. It challenges the
wedge structurally rather than scoring an assumption.*

**What he actually said, one line each:**

- At the **established** restaurant: *"at [Halden's] we can look back and figure it out like i know okay we
  should have this many [X] sandwiches prepped for a tuesday."* — **Solved**, by lookback, held as a par.
- At the **new** restaurant: *"here it's a little uh we're still figuring it out it's so new"*, and *"it's
  only been two weeks that we've been reopened. So it's a little bit of a discovery period."* — **Unsolved.**
- On item-level demand generally: *"whatever you don't have prepared… that's what people are going to
  order… And whatever you have a lot of, you never sell. **Somehow the public senses your weaknesses.**"*

**Why this is a problem for us.** Our engine learns a demand distribution from history. Where Wes *has*
history he has already extracted the answer and holds it for free, zero-maintenance, in a form he trusts
completely. Where he lacks history — and feels the pain acutely — **we lack it too.** The pain and the data
are inversely distributed. A model trained on two weeks of a brand-new BBQ restaurant during a World Cup
distortion has nothing to say that his gut doesn't say better.

**Three honest responses, ordered by how much I'd trust them:**

1. **Retarget.** The buyer isn't the exciting new opening; it's the **mature site with history the operator
   has stopped mining** — especially where prep counts have been **delegated to a manager** whose pars are
   not the owner's (the Nadia case in §5). A second person's pars, unvalidated against history that
   *exists*, is a real and unexamined gap, and the strongest opening this call produced. It was never explored.
2. **Sell against the par, not against nothing.** A1 needs restating across the study: the competitor is not
   "no tool," it's a **free, trusted, zero-maintenance par level**. Beating it means beating an experienced
   operator's memory in realized dollars — testable on backtest, and never yet framed as the baseline. Per
   standing order #3, **the par sheet is the baseline to beat.**
3. **Cold-start as a product surface** (weakest — flag as potential drift). Pooling across similar venues to
   forecast a new opening is technically interesting and exactly the sophistication the Anti-Drift Standing
   Order warns about. **Do not build this on one anecdote.**
